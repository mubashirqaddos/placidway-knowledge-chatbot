# Known limitations and one more week of improvements

The assistant is deliberately restricted to the seven assessment URLs. It cannot book appointments, check live clinic availability, diagnose, recommend a treatment or confirm safety for an individual's medical history. Published descriptions of alternative therapies are treated as website claims, not proof of medical efficacy. Missing data leads to an explicit fallback rather than a guessed price or recovery time.

The initial HTML extraction was checked for readable price tables and package context on all seven sources. The crawler does not fetch additional video pages, transcripts or dynamic endpoints. A site redesign or client-rendered price change could invalidate extraction; refresh then retains the previous snapshot and reports failure. Prices are snapshots, may change, and are not binding personalized quotes. Broad alternative-medicine listings often describe different services, so a country ranking is inappropriate without matched procedure, currency, duration and inclusions.

Retrieval combines BM25 and local English embeddings with RRF. Gemini translates non-English search queries and can summarize in the user's language, but translation and short follow-up resolution can fail. Relevance thresholds are heuristic. Verbatim evidence, source checks and per-claim number validation reduce hallucinations but do not prove that every paraphrase is semantically entailed. Financial replies therefore show original evidence. Excerpt mode is more conservative and does not provide full multilingual summaries or general comparisons.

Free Gemini quotas can interrupt service; retries are bounded and there is no automatic paid fallback. Free Streamlit hosting can hibernate after twelve idle hours, so it does not guarantee uninterrupted seven-day uptime. Files/logs can disappear on redeployment; refreshes persist only for the running deployment unless the owner updates the repository snapshot. Quote details remain a session-local draft and are not submitted automatically. The Google free tier's data terms mean this demo should not receive private medical records.

With another week, I would build a larger independently reviewed evaluation set (including misleading prices, multilingual follow-ups and retrieved prompt injection), calibrate retrieval and evidence checks against it, and add a small local reranker if CPU limits permit. I would improve structured price records and conflict detection across pages, then add a strictly allowlisted, robots-compliant browser extraction fallback only if real source changes require it. Finally I would evaluate a durable, consent-based lead destination and hosting that meets the reviewer's exact availability expectations without introducing unauthorized cost.
